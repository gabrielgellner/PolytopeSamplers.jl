PolytopeSamplers.jl

PolytopeSamplers.jl draws points from the uniform distribution, or from a density, on a convex polytope

\[P = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \ell \le x \le u,\ A x \le b,\ C x = d\}\]

by Markov chain Monte Carlo, and reports how far the results can be trusted: effective sample sizes, R̂ and Monte Carlo standard errors for every coordinate.

It offers the billiard walk, coordinate hit-and-run with rounding (CHRR), hit-and-run, artificial centering hit-and-run (ACHR), the Dikin and Vaidya walks, and constrained Riemannian Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (CRHMC). Each is calibrated against exact answers and stress-tested on hard geometry; see How the samplers are tested.

The package began as HRMCMC.jl, a sampler for the interaction strengths of food webs consistent with an equilibrium (Example: constrained food webs).

Installation

using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/gabrielgellner/PolytopeSamplers.jl")

The package needs Julia 1.13. Linear programs are solved with HiGHS through JuMP.

Quick start

Define the polytope, then sample until the chains have converged:

using PolytopeSamplers, Random

# The triangle x ≥ 0, x₁ + x₂ ≤ 1
P = Polytope([0.0, 0.0], [1.0, 1.0]; A = [1.0 1.0], b = [1.0])

r = sample_until_converged(Xoshiro(1), warmup(P); target_ess = 1_000)
r.converged, r.n
(true, 1000)

sample_until_converged runs four BilliardWalk chains, doubling their length, until every coordinate has an effective sample size of at least target_ess and R̂ below 1.01. The estimates come with their Monte Carlo standard errors:

d = r.diagnostics
d.mean, d.mcse_mean
([0.3320434594303185, 0.3279621418180781], [0.005069327039143876, 0.005295040233448698])

The exact means are 1/3. The points themselves are the columns of the chains:

X = reduce(hcat, r.chains)
size(X)
(2, 4000)
