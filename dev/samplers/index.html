Choosing a sampler

Use BilliardWalk unless you sample a density, in which case use CoordinateHitAndRun or, with a gradient, CRHMC. The other methods are independent checks. The tables below give the measurements behind this advice; all ran on one thread of a 10-core Apple machine, to 1000 effective samples on rounded polytopes unless noted.

Robustness

  • Corners. From starting points near the corners of a 49-dimensional simplex, the billiard walk converged in 1000 steps and CRHMC in 10 000; hit-and-run, CHRR, ACHR and the barrier walks had not converged after 10 000.
  • Vertices. A CHRR chain started exactly at a vertex of a simplex never moves, as no coordinate axis points into the polytope there. The samplers start chains in the interior.
  • Rounding. Without rounding, the billiard walk, hit-and-run and CHRR stall on long thin polytopes, and CRHMC's metric becomes too ill-conditioned for double precision near facets. The Dikin and Vaidya walks behave the same with or without rounding. Keep the default rounding.
  • ACHR is only approximately Markov: its directions depend on the history of the chain.

Parameters

  • BilliardWalk(; trajectory_length, max_reflections): the mean trajectory length defaults to 2√k r for inscribed radius r (about 1 after rounding).
  • CoordinateHitAndRun(; moves, scan): moves per step (default k) and the order of coordinates (:cyclic, the default, or :random).
  • DikinWalk(; r), VaidyaWalk(; r): the proposal radius, 0.5 and 0.7 by default, giving acceptance rates of 0.5 to 0.65.
  • CRHMC(; initial_step_size, effective_step_size, max_ode_steps, implicit_tol): the step size adapts by itself.
