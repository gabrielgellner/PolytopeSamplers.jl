Volume

volume estimates the volume of a polytope with a standard error. For a polytope with equalities it gives the volume of the slice the polytope lies on, in that slice's own dimension: the simplex x ≥ 0, Σx = 1 in 3 variables has area √3 / 2.

using PolytopeSamplers, Random

P = Polytope(zeros(3), ones(3); C = ones(1, 3), d = [1.0])
v = volume(Xoshiro(1), P)
v.volume, v.log_stderr, sqrt(3) / 2
(0.8372126255660302, 0.05329786415571778, 0.8660254037844386)

log_stderr is the standard error of log_volume, close to the relative standard error of volume. Volumes in high dimensions under- or overflow, so work with log_volume:

cube = Polytope(zeros(50), fill(2.0, 50))
v = volume(Xoshiro(2), cube; error = 0.05)
v.log_volume, v.log_stderr, 50 * log(2)
(34.61248582328554, 0.04030217101114115, 34.657359027997266)

Method

The estimate multiplies volume ratios (multiphase Monte Carlo with cooling balls, Chalkis, Emiris & Fisikopoulos 2023). In the rounded coordinates of the warmup, balls centered at the warmup center shrink from one holding the polytope to one of which 10–15% lies inside it. Each body K ∩ B(ρᵢ₊₁) holds about an eighth of K ∩ B(ρᵢ), so a 63-dimensional food web polytope needs about 10 phases.

  • The share of the last ball inside the polytope comes from independent uniform points of the ball.
  • Each ratio vol(K ∩ B(ρᵢ₊₁)) / vol(K ∩ B(ρᵢ)) comes from coordinate hit-and-run chains on K ∩ B(ρᵢ), doubled until R̂ is below 1.01 and the ratio's Monte Carlo standard error meets its share of error.

The phases are independent, so the variance of the log volume is the sum of the squared relative standard errors of the ratios.

Calibration

Over 50 seeds on a 40-dimensional cube, a 20-dimensional simplex, an 8-dimensional cross-polytope and a 14-dimensional simplex slice, the z-scores of the log volume had root mean square between 0.90 and 1.13, and 46 to 50 of the 50 estimates were within two standard errors. The mean z-score was between 0.0 and 0.27: the logarithm of a ratio estimate is biased by about half its squared relative error, small next to the standard error.

Food webs

For constrained food webs with fixed growth rates, the volume of the polytope of interaction strengths measures how many communities share the equilibrium. Under a uniform prior on the growth rates and interaction strengths jointly, each growth-rate vector is weighted by this volume.
