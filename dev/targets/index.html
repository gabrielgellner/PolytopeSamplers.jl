Non-uniform targets

Target is the distribution on a polytope with density proportional to exp(ℓ(x)). ℓ is a function of the vector x, or any object implementing LogDensityProblems.

A Dirichlet distribution on the simplex

The Dirichlet distribution with parameters α has log density Σ (αᵢ - 1) log xᵢ on the simplex, and Beta marginals with means αᵢ / Σα:

using PolytopeSamplers, Random

α = [2.0, 3.0, 4.0]
P = Polytope(zeros(3), ones(3); C = ones(1, 3), d = [1.0])
t = Target(P, x -> sum((α .- 1) .* log.(x)); gradient = (g, x) -> (g .= (α .- 1) ./ x))

r = sample_until_converged(Xoshiro(1), t, CoordinateHitAndRun(); target_ess = 2_000)
r.diagnostics.mean, α ./ sum(α)
([0.2214207226754889, 0.3323523397235175, 0.4462269376009936], [0.2222222222222222, 0.3333333333333333, 0.4444444444444444])

Gaussian and exponential targets

GaussianTarget restricts a Gaussian with mean μ and covariance Σ to the polytope, and ExponentialTarget has density proportional to exp(-cᵀx). On a chord their densities are a truncated normal and a truncated exponential, which HitAndRun and CoordinateHitAndRun draw exactly, by the rejection samplers of Robert (1995), without evaluating ℓ:

box = Polytope(fill(-1.0, 2), fill(1.0, 2))
t = GaussianTarget(box, [0.5, 0.0], [1.0 0.8; 0.8 1.0])
r = sample_until_converged(Xoshiro(3), t, CoordinateHitAndRun(); target_ess = 2_000)
r.diagnostics.mean, r.diagnostics.mcse_mean
([0.23232855134467695, -0.12582087094235872], [0.009271626486352208, 0.009733899487122409])

On a 10-dimensional box, coordinate hit-and-run gave 6 times the effective samples per second of slice sampling on the same Gaussian, and 13 times on an exponential density; hit-and-run gave 1.5 times on the Gaussian and the same on the exponential, where computing the chord dominates its step. Both targets are LogDensityProblems objects with gradients, so DikinWalk, VaidyaWalk and CRHMC take them too.

Robert, C. P. (1995). Simulation of truncated normal variables. Statistics and Computing 5, 121–125. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00143942

Which samplers support densities

  • HitAndRun and CoordinateHitAndRun draw each point on a chord from the density along it by slice sampling with shrinkage (Neal 2003), starting from the whole chord, or exactly for GaussianTarget and ExponentialTarget. Slice sampling is exact for any ℓ and needs no tuning; it calls ℓ a few times per move.
  • DikinWalk and VaidyaWalk add ℓ(z') - ℓ(z) to their Metropolis ratio.
  • CRHMC adds -ℓ to its potential and needs the gradient of ℓ: pass gradient = (g, x) -> ..., which fills g with ∇ℓ(x), or a LogDensityProblems object of order 1, for example one built with LogDensityProblemsAD.jl.
  • BilliardWalk and ACHR sample only the uniform distribution and throw an ArgumentError for a target.

The default method of sample_until_converged for a target is HitAndRun; CoordinateHitAndRun was faster on the targets tested.

A LogDensityProblems object

using LogDensityProblems

struct Gaussian
    μ::Vector{Float64}
end
LogDensityProblems.dimension(g::Gaussian) = length(g.μ)
LogDensityProblems.capabilities(::Type{Gaussian}) = LogDensityProblems.LogDensityOrder{1}()
LogDensityProblems.logdensity(g::Gaussian, x) = -sum(abs2, x .- g.μ) / 2
LogDensityProblems.logdensity_and_gradient(g::Gaussian, x) = (-sum(abs2, x .- g.μ) / 2, g.μ .- x)

box = Polytope(fill(-1.0, 2), fill(1.0, 2))
r = sample_until_converged(Xoshiro(2), Target(box, Gaussian([0.5, 0.0])), CRHMC(); target_ess = 1_000)
r.diagnostics.mean, r.diagnostics.mcse_mean
([0.1290282662527416, -0.0019687376984104912], [0.015633759126739158, 0.0156515248610681])

Truncated to [-1, 1], the exact means are 0.5 - (φ(0.5) - φ(-1.5)) / (Φ(0.5) - Φ(-1.5)) ≈ 0.144 and 0, for the standard normal density φ and distribution function Φ.

Calibration

Over 20 seeds, on a Dirichlet distribution on a simplex, truncated exponentials on a box with rates 1, 5 and 20, and a Gaussian with correlation 0.8, every supported sampler had z-scores of its means and standard deviations against the exact values with root mean square between 0.73 and 1.15.

ℓ is called from every chain, on separate threads when Julia has them, so it must be safe to call concurrently.
